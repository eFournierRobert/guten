There was a piece of pine leaning against the wall for months. Too short for the project I'd bought it for, too good to throw away. Last weekend I finally gave it a job.

The shelf is narrow, just deep enough for a postcard, a little ceramic bowl, and the stone I brought back from the coast. Nothing on it is especially valuable. All of it is worth keeping.

## An afternoon, not a project plan

I kept the process simple:

- Measure the awkward space beside the desk.
- Cut the board a little shorter than that.
- Sand the edges until they feel good in the hand.
- Wipe on a little oil and let it dry.
- Fit two brackets, checking the wall fixings first.

The hardest part was deciding where to put it. I held it against three different walls before returning to the first one.

## Enough is a useful measure

There is a temptation to turn every small task into a larger one. Build a whole bookcase. Reorganise the room. Learn a new joint.

This time I made one shelf. It holds the things I wanted it to hold, and the leftover pine is no longer leaning against the wall. I'll take that.
