I usually take the shortest route. Two busy streets, one crossing, the same corner shop. It is efficient enough that I barely remember walking it.

On Sunday I turned toward the river instead. The path was damp, the trees mostly bare. Someone had left a neat stack of stones on the low wall by the water. I stopped to look, which is something I rarely do on my usual route.

## No particular destination

Without headphones, a walk has a different texture. Bicycle tyres on wet leaves. A dog drinking noisily from a puddle. The small pause before a conversation resumes as you pass.

I didn't come back with a revelation. Just cold hands and a better sense of the weather. That felt like enough.

> A longer route can be a way of giving the day a little more room.

Next time, I might take the same path. Or the street behind the library. There are plenty of ways home that I haven't tried yet.
